# Losses, learned evaluators and evaluation metrics for professional green-screen video matting (CorridorKey 2.0), as of October 2026

Scope note: about 15 tool calls. Primary sources fetched: MatAnyone 2 (arXiv 2512.11782 v2 HTML and CVPR 2026 supplementary), MatAnyone 1 (arXiv 2501.14677), RVM (arXiv 2108.11515 and train_loss.py), FBA Matting (arXiv 2003.07711), Rhemann 2009, Erofeev 2015, Zhang et al. attention-guided video matting (arXiv 2105.11427), MODNet (AAAI 2022), HDR training papers. Several requested items had no retrievable evidence; they are listed under Gaps in each section rather than guessed. Statements marked INFERENCE are my own reasoning, not sourced.

## 1. Which matting losses have proven gains (ablation evidence)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced alpha loss set is L1 + 5-level Laplacian pyramid (+ composition loss for image matting), which is exactly what RVM, MatAnyone, MatteFormer, AEMatter and ViTMatte converge on. The only controlled loss ablation I could retrieve (FBA Matting) shows composition and Laplacian help, and the gradient loss did NOT help. Evidence for perceptual/LPIPS/DINO, GAN, FFT, connectivity-aware and uncertainty-weighted losses in matting was not found.

### Cited Findings
- FBA Matting ablation on Composition-1k (20 epochs): L1 only: MSE 11.2 / SAD 36.3 / Grad 14.9 / Conn 32.5. Adding composition loss: 9.1 / 34.5 / 15.0 / 31.3 (improves all except Grad). Adding Laplacian: 7.4 / 33.5 / 12.9 / 28.5 ("significant reduction in errors across all metrics"). Adding the gradient loss (Tang et al.): 8.1 / 36.3 / 13.8 / 32.0, i.e. worse on every metric; authors note this was also reported by another paper but kept it in later models anyway and left it to future work. — [FBA Matting](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.07711v1)
- FBA final loss: L1 + comp + gradient + Laplacian on alpha, plus 0.25 x (L1, Laplacian, composition, and a gradient exclusion loss) on F and B. Their later models add an alpha clipping variant (clip_alpha) which improved SAD 32.8 to 31.2 and MSE 7.2 to 6.9 (model 6 vs 7; details of clip_alpha truncated in my fetch). Batch size 1 with GroupNorm + weight standardization was much better than batch 6 with BatchNorm (SAD 36.2 to 32.8). — [FBA Matting](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.07711v1)
- RVM loss (code): alpha L1 + Laplacian (5 levels, level weight 2^level, divided by levels) + 5 x MSE on the frame-to-frame alpha difference; foreground L1 and 5 x MSE temporal coherence, both only on pixels where GT alpha > 0. Paper says Laplacian + L1 "reported to produce the best result" citing earlier works; RVM publishes no loss-term ablation. — [RVM train_loss.py](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/train_loss.py), [RVM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.11515)
- MatAnyone 1 matting loss (per my fetch summary of the HTML): L_mat = L1 + 5 x L_lap + L_tc, with the Laplacian pyramid weights 2^(s-1)/5, s=1..5; plus a core-area L1 and a scaled DDC boundary loss for segmentation-only data, combined as L_cs = L_core + 1.5 x L_boundary. Scaled DDC loss: L_boundary = mean over pixels i of sum over top-k color-nearest neighbors j of | (alpha_i - alpha_j)(F - B) - ||I_i - I_j||_2 |, with F and B approximated by the mean of top-k largest and smallest values in a window. The scaling fixes "segmentation-like jagged and stair-stepped edges" of vanilla DDC; shown only qualitatively. Note: MatAnyone 2 writes the Laplacian term without the factor 5, so the "5x" weight needs checking against the released code. — [MatAnyone](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14677v2), [MatAnyone 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- MatAnyone 1 stage schedule: stage 1 85K iters on VM800 (AdamW, lr 1e-4, batch 16, seq len 3 then 8); stage 2 40K iters, lr 1e-5, adds core supervision; stage 3 5K iters image matting data, lr 1e-6. Table 3 (real 1080p benchmark): baseline MAD 3.16 / MSE 2.65 / dtSSD 1.37; +new data 2.55 / 2.25 / 1.36; +consistent memory propagation 1.85 / 1.67 / 1.25; +new training scheme (core + boundary supervision from segmentation data) 0.42 / 0.34 / 0.94. Caveat: MAD against SAM-style pseudo-GT core regions, so this mostly measures semantic stability, not edge quality. — [MatAnyone](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14677v2)
- MatteFormer: L1 + composition + Laplacian (no per-loss ablation). AEMatter: L1 + Charbonnier L1 (unknown region) + Laplacian, trained on 1024x1024 patches ("larger patches improve context aggregation"). ViTMatte: L1 computed separately over known and unknown trimap regions, + Laplacian + gradient penalty; no per-loss ablation in retrieved text. — [MatteFormer](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.15662), [AEMatter](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.01171), [ViTMatte](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15272v2)
- Survey notes that L1/composition/Laplacian are the standard set, that gradient loss was proposed because other losses "cannot adequately promote sharpness ... resulting in blurred boundaries," and that some works use L1 on the transition region and MSE on solid regions. — [Deep Image Matting survey](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04672)
- MODNet ablation on PPM-100 (MSE / MAD): none 0.0162 / 0.0235; with semantic loss 0.0097 / 0.0158; + detail loss 0.0083 / 0.0142; + e-ASPP 0.0057 / 0.0109; + supervised-segmentation pretraining 0.0044 / 0.0086 (cumulative reading of the check marks, which are partly garbled in the extraction). Supports explicit auxiliary supervision on a coarse/semantic branch and a detail branch, which matches the user's base head and /2 auxiliary head design. — [MODNet](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v36i1.19999)
- BiRefNet (segmentation, not matting) uses BCE 30 : IoU 0.5 : SSIM 10 : CE 5 "to keep all the losses on the same quantitative level"; the repo's Matting config uses bce 30, mae 100, ssim 10 and sets IoU to 0. I did not retrieve a numeric ablation table. SSIM loss is labelled as the boundary-level term. — [BiRefNet paper](https://doi.org/10.26599/air.2024.9150038), [BiRefNet config.py](https://github.com/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet/blob/main/config.py)
- Boundary-aware weighting in segmentation (structure loss, from the BiRefNet loss.py): weights 1 + 5 x |avgpool31(target) - target| on BCE and IoU, i.e. 6x weight at edges. A cheap edge-weighting recipe; no matting ablation found. — [BiRefNet loss.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/multimodalart/LHM/blob/main/engine/BiRefNet/loss.py)
- Adversarial losses exist in matting (AlphaGAN, Background Matting v1, adversarially-guided portrait matting 2023) but I retrieved no ablation numbers. — [AlphaGAN](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326632662_AlphaGAN_Generative_adversarial_networks_for_natural_image_matting), [Background Matting](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.04433v1), [Adversarially-Guided Portrait Matting](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.02981)
- Alpha-label-free training: DDC loss (AAAI 2025) with only trimaps matches a fine-label-supervised baseline on AM-2K and P3M-10K and "sometimes offers even more satisfying results than human-labeled ground truth." — [Training Matting Models Without Alpha Labels](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/32597/34752)
- MODNet SOC: self-supervised sub-objective consistency fine-tune on ~50,000 unlabeled real frames, 15 epochs, BatchNorm frozen, lr 1e-4, with a frozen copy regularizer to keep detail; qualitative only. — [MODNet](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v36i1.19999)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Your current set (L1, 5-level Laplacian, composition, FG L1, temporal L1) matches the best-evidenced recipe. Adding a separate gradient loss is not supported (FBA shows it hurt); prefer to get edge sharpness from the Laplacian pyramid and the edge-band weighted L1.
- INFERENCE: Composition loss over random backgrounds gave the smaller of the two gains in FBA (SAD 36.3 to 34.5) but it is the term that couples alpha and F to what the compositor sees, so keep it as a primary term and make it the thing you also use for unlabeled-data consistency (Section 3).
- INFERENCE: A BiRefNet-style edge weight (1 + 5 x edge indicator, 6x) on the alpha L1 is the cheapest candidate for "edge-weighted loss"; treat it as an A/B test, not a proven gain for matting.

### Gaps
- No retrievable ablation for: perceptual/LPIPS/DINO feature losses on alpha or composites, FFT/frequency losses, connectivity-aware losses, uncertainty-weighted (heteroscedastic) losses, or GAN losses in matting. DiffMatte's loss ablation was not retrieved. BiRefNet and ViTMatte per-loss tables were not retrieved. I cannot claim gains or harm for any of these.
- RVM paper contains no loss ablation; MatAnyone's exact L_lap weight (1 vs 5) is inconsistent between my fetch summary and MatAnyone 2's text; verify in released code.

## 2. Temporal losses and temporal metrics

### Takeaway
Plain frame-difference losses (dtSSD-style: match predicted temporal difference to GT temporal difference) give consistent gains. Using optical-flow-warped differences (MESSDdt) as a TRAINING loss hurt in at least one paper because flow is noisy on translucent pixels, even though motion-aware versions correlate better with humans as a METRIC. For humans, temporal coherence matters more than per-frame accuracy, except at high motion speed.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang et al. (attention-guided temporally coherent video matting): temporal coherence term L_tc = |(alpha_{t-1} - alpha_t) - (GT_{t-1} - GT_t)|_1 on the unknown region, i.e. dtSSD as a loss. Adding only L_tc "obtain[s] performance boost across all metrics, except pixel-wise alpha accuracy in narrow trimaps"; they explicitly did not use MESSDdt as the loss because "even computed with SOTA optical flow algorithms (RAFT) there are severe motion noises for translucent pixels, which hurts the performance of the network." Numeric supplement not retrieved. — [Attention-guided Temporally Coherent Video Object Matting](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.11427)
- Erofeev et al. (BMVC 2015) subjective study of 12 methods on 10 sequences: "alpha temporal coherence is significantly more important to human perception of video-matting quality than accuracy"; motion-aware metrics (MESSDdt, MEdtSSD, flow-based) raise correlation further despite imperfect optical flow; the relative inferiority of accuracy metrics appears mostly for slow-motion sequences and "becomes almost insignificant as motion speed increases." Correlation computed as Pearson on the log of each score. Exact correlation values not retrieved (PDF extraction failed). — [Erofeev et al. 2015](https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2015/papers/paper099/paper099.pdf), [videomatting.com paper](https://videomatting.com/content/paper/videoMattingBenchmark.pdf)
- Erofeev metric definitions: dtSSD compares temporal derivatives of alpha vs GT; MESSDdt warps by motion vectors; both "capture unexpected alpha temporal changes and ignore temporally coherent errors." Their GT flow was computed on the GT sequence composed over solid green. — [Erofeev et al. 2015](https://videomatting.com/content/paper/videoMattingBenchmark.pdf)
- MatAnyone 2 (L_tc restricted to pixels reliable in both consecutive frames) with MQE: dtSSD 1.65 to 1.63 from online MQE loss alone, to 1.54 with VMReal, 1.53 with reference frames (YouTubeMatte 1080p). So dtSSD gains came mostly from data, not loss. — [MatAnyone 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- MatAnyone 1: architecture (alpha memory bank, region-adaptive memory fusion trained with a BCE loss on |alpha_{t-1} - alpha_t| >= delta) reduced dtSSD 1.36 to 1.25, bigger than any loss effect shown. — [MatAnyone](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14677v2)
- MODNet One-Frame-Delay: a post-process that replaces a pixel with the average of frames t-1 and t+1 if those two agree and t differs from both; removes boundary flicker. — [MODNet](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v36i1.19999)
- RVM uses MSE (not L1) on the frame-difference with weight 5, for both alpha and FG. — [RVM train_loss.py](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/train_loss.py)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Keep the temporal L1 on alpha frame-differences (L1 is more robust to the genuine large changes at occlusion edges than RVM's MSE). Weight in the 1x to 5x range of the main L1 per RVM/MatAnyone, but gate it: apply only where both frames are reliable (MatAnyone 2) and only on differences in the GT, not zero-motion regions, to avoid over-smoothing real motion blur.
- INFERENCE: Do not use flow-warped consistency as a training loss on alpha edges (noisy flow on translucent pixels), but use a flow-warped metric (MESSDdt or a warped-difference variant) in evaluation, computed with GT flow from the synthetic clean plate when possible. Optionally use flow-warped consistency only in opaque-core regions for unlabeled real clips.
- INFERENCE: Because humans penalize temporal incoherence more at low motion speeds, report dtSSD stratified by motion magnitude, particularly for near-static subjects with edge chatter.

### Gaps
- Numeric correlations between each temporal metric and human scores (Erofeev) not retrieved. No ablation on flow-warped training losses with numbers. No evidence found about the perceptual effect of L1 vs L2 temporal losses.

## 3. Learned quality evaluators and using unlabeled real footage

### Takeaway
MatAnyone 2's Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) is the only published learned no-reference matting evaluator found. It is a small frozen-style network (DINOv3 encoder + DPT decoder) that outputs a per-pixel reliable/erroneous map, used (a) as an online loss L_eval weighted 0.1 plus a mask on the normal matting losses, and (b) offline to fuse two models' mattes into pseudo-GT. It gives modest but consistent gains in their ablation. It is portrait/segmentation-mask-centric and coarse, so a green-screen version should be retrained on your own domain.

### Cited Findings
- Architecture and inputs: input is the tuple (RGB frame, predicted alpha, segmentation mask); output is a binary evaluation map (1 = reliable, 0 = erroneous). Encoder is pretrained DINOv3, decoder is DPT ("inspired by Depth Anything"). The segmentation mask input is "crucial": for core regions the MQE inherits semantics from the mask, for boundary regions it uses the mask to focus on fine matting quality. — [MatAnyone 2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2), [Supplementary](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Yang_MatAnyone_2_Scaling_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)
- Training: formulated as binary segmentation. Labels are synthesized from P3M-10k: take an alpha prediction, split into a 7x7 grid of patches, per-patch discrepancy D = 0.9 x MAD + 0.1 x Grad, min-max normalize to [0,1], label reliable if D < 0.2. Loss: focal (gamma 2, alpha 0.25) + Dice to handle class imbalance. 8 x A800-80G, batch 16, 80K iterations, lr 3e-6 for the encoder and 10x for the decoder. — [Supplementary](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Yang_MatAnyone_2_Scaling_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)
- Online use: L_eval = ||P_eval||_1 where P_eval is the MQE's per-pixel error probability; total matting loss L_total = L_mat^M + 0.1 x L_eval (0.1 chosen to keep magnitudes comparable). Crucially, the reliability map R_t = 1[M_eval = 1] also masks the L1, Laplacian, and temporal-coherence losses, so unreliable pixels of pseudo-GT are excluded from supervision. L1 is normalized by the number of reliable pixels. Claimed to be a "more effective and stable learning signal for boundary regions" than MatAnyone 1's weak unsupervised loss. — [MatAnyone 2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- Offline use (annotation pipeline): two branches, video model B_V (MatAnyone, first-frame mask) as the stable base and image model B_I (MattePro prompted by SAM 2 per frame). Fusion mask M_fuse = M_eval_I x (1 - M_eval_V), Gaussian-blurred (9x9, sigma 5), alpha = alpha_V (1 - M_fuse) + alpha_I M_fuse; fused eval map = union. Redundant masks removed at IoU > 0.9. Result: VMReal, 28K clips / 2.4M frames (4.5K 1080p clips from stock sites and YouTube, rest from SA-V at ~720p). — [MatAnyone 2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- Ablation, YouTubeMatte 1080p (MAD / MSE / Grad / dtSSD): baseline 1.99 / 0.71 / 8.91 / 1.65; + L_eval 1.90 / 0.62 / 8.20 / 1.63; + VMReal data 1.76 / 0.61 / 7.65 / 1.54; + reference-frame training 1.61 / 0.50 / 7.13 / 1.53. So online MQE loss alone gives about 4.5% MAD, 13% MSE, 8% Grad; the data pipeline adds roughly twice as much again. Training RVM on VMReal also helps (1080p MAD 4.27 to 3.37, MSE 2.25 to 1.51). Conn not in the ablation table. — [MatAnyone 2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- Use as a judge on real footage: MQE-based metrics ERR (fraction of pixels flagged), MER (within foreground), BER (within a dilated-minus-eroded boundary band). On a real benchmark MatAnyone vs MatAnyone 2: ERR 0.62 vs 0.46, MER 1.69 vs 1.13, BER 20.23 vs 15.19 (percent) while MAD/MSE are equal at 0.19/0.13 and dtSSD improves 1.08 to 0.99. Reference metrics on real footage only work in core regions with SAM 2 masks as pseudo-GT, so they miss the boundary. Authors say MQE trends "generally align" with reference metrics on synthetic sets. — [MatAnyone 2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- Stated limitations: pipeline bounded by the matting models it fuses; closed-loop refinement left for future work; MQE "saturates toward 1.0 for highly unreliable patches, making it conservative there." — same source (limitations from my fetch summary of the HTML).
- Older no-reference alternatives: gradient-correlation local matte evaluation (ICPR 2010) measuring local gradient correlation between image and matte without GT. Not learned. — [Yao et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/icpr.2010.90)
- Related unlabeled-data objectives: DDC distance consistency loss (alpha propagation from known regions), MODNet SOC consistency, both listed in Section 1.
- CorridorKey (original): outputs straight un-multiplied FG color and linear alpha from green plates, 2048x2048 backbone, 16/32-bit linear EXR in/out, despill expectations. — [CorridorKey repo](https://github.com/nikopueringer/corridorkey)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE (design for CK2): Train a CorridorKey-specific critic on the composite-error signal you can compute exactly in synthetic data. Generate labels by perturbing GT: use your model's own predictions across checkpoints, plus synthetic corruptions (erode/dilate, chatter, holes, spill residue). Label per-patch with a composite-based discrepancy (composite MAE over several new backgrounds, plus edge-band and hole terms) instead of MatAnyone's 0.9 MAD + 0.1 Grad on a 7x7 grid, which is very coarse for hair and 4K.
- INFERENCE: The critic input for green screen should be (plate, predicted alpha, predicted FG, optional coarse hint mask) because spill is visible only by comparing FG to plate. Output both a reliable/erroneous map and a regression of expected per-pixel composite error (continuous), since a binary map saturates and gives vanishing gradients near the decision boundary.
- INFERENCE: On real, no-GT clips use the critic exactly like MatAnyone 2: (1) mask the standard L1/Laplacian/temporal losses by the reliability map when the target is a pseudo-label from a teacher or ensemble; (2) add a small 0.1-weight critic penalty on the student's output; (3) use the critic offline to fuse pseudo-labels from several keyers/teachers (including a classic keyer like Keylight/IBK output and a heavier teacher) pixel-wise. Keep the critic frozen and refresh it periodically with new failure cases to limit reward hacking (a model can learn to fool a frozen critic; this risk is not discussed in the paper, so treat it as a hypothesis).
- INFERENCE: Because the fused improvements in MatAnyone 2 came more from data than from L_eval (13% MSE vs a further 2x from data/reference frames), the highest-value use of the critic for CK2 is offline curation/fusion of the real-footage pseudo-GT, with the online loss as a small add-on.
- INFERENCE: Never use the same critic as both a training signal and the only judge. Hold out a separately trained critic (different seed/encoder, e.g. SAM-family vs DINOv3) and human review for judging.

### Gaps
- Exact MQE parameter count, whether DINOv3 is frozen or fine-tuned (supplement says lr 3e-6 for encoder, so fine-tuned), and any human validation of MQE agreement were not found. No published study of learned no-reference matting quality assessment other than MQE found in my searches (searched generally; absence of evidence). No evidence on using SAM or DINOv3 as a frozen perceptual loss for alpha. No evidence on adversarial hacking of MQE.

## 4. Loss space: linear vs sRGB vs log/asinh for HDR; foreground losses and spill

### Takeaway
For HDR/linear data, the evidence says compress before computing the loss; training in linear with plain L1/L2 lets the brightest pixels dominate and costs 2 to 9 dB versus display-encoded training in a controlled study. asinh (your current FG choice) is a reasonable member of that family, though I found no matting paper that compares transfer functions. For spill, I found no published loss that directly penalizes residual spill; related work uses synthetic spill-free GT and custom losses separating mixed vs spilled pixels.

### Cited Findings
- Ke et al. (CVPR Workshops 2025, "Training Neural Networks on RAW and HDR Images for Restoration Tasks") tested PQ, PU21, mu-law display encoding vs linear training with perceptual-correction losses on denoising, deblurring, super-resolution: networks "train significantly better on HDR and RAW images represented in display-encoded color spaces," gain of 2 to 9 dB. — [Ke et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvprw67362.2025.00085)
- HDRUNet: Tanh-L1 loss (compress with tanh before L1) beat plain L1/L2; it improved PSNR-mu by 0.35 dB at some cost to PSNR-L because plain L1/L2 weight bright values more and "the network mainly focuses on the highlight areas." L1 beat L2 on both PSNR-L and PSNR-mu. Plain L1/L2 also performed badly at denoising well-exposed regions. — [HDRUNet](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2105.13084)
- ArtHDR-Net: losses between mu-law tone-mapped prediction and GT "to avoid the high-intensity values to dominate the loss"; L1 + VGG perceptual with weights 0.1 and 0.5; says L1-only produces visually degraded HDRs; avoids L2 due to outlier sensitivity. — [ArtHDR-Net](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03827)
- Hybrid HDR loss paper applies VGG perceptual loss on log-transformed HDR inputs because the VGG is trained on LDR images. — [Hybrid loss for single-image HDR](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.07134)
- RVM and other matting papers compute FG L1 only where GT alpha > 0 (RVM) or on masks; FBA applies 0.25-weighted L1/Laplacian/composition/gradient-exclusion losses on F and B, where the exclusion loss multiplies the gradient magnitudes of F and B to discourage both having edges at the same place. — [RVM train_loss.py](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/train_loss.py), [FBA Matting](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.07711v1)
- Green-screen-specific: Maul's thesis builds a Blender-rendered dataset with spill-free GT FG and spilled renders and trains a U-Net with a "custom loss function which reinforces the difference between mixed and spilled pixels" (custom accuracy up to ~80%); the key difficulty is distinguishing mixed pixels (should become translucent) from spilled pixels (should be recolored). A 2022 Visual Computer paper (Automatic and real-time green screen keying) builds a green-screen dataset with alpha, FG and spill samples and an end-to-end net for keying + despill. Hachmann and Rosenhahn (2023) report superior spill compensation from a dual-backdrop network. — [Maul thesis](https://www.szi.uni-bremen.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/thesis-pmaul_compressed.pdf), [Automatic and real-time green screen keying](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00371-022-02542-x), [Color-aware Deep Temporal Backdrop Duplex Matting](https://doi.org/10.1145/3587819.3590973)
- Magenta Green Screen (2023) produces clean real alpha + color by recording on green with red/blue-only lighting and ML colorization; the authors argue it supplies much higher quality training data for matting. — [Magenta Green Screen](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.13702)
- VFX practice on despill: check despill affects only the necessary areas, is not too strong or weak, introduces no discolouration (often magenta), and that real blues/greens (eyes, wardrobe) survive; check for dark/light edges and halos. — [Keheka tech check](https://www.keheka.com/tech-check-checklist/), [CompStart tech check](https://compstart.pro/tech-check)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Keep the FG loss in asinh space but with a scale parameter chosen so that the diffuse-white region (scene-linear 0.18 to 1) is in the near-linear part of asinh and highlights above ~4 are compressed; the Ke et al. and HDRUNet results imply this should beat linear L1. Verify with a one-run A/B (asinh vs mu-law vs sRGB-OETF-extended) since no matting-specific study exists.
- INFERENCE: Compute the composition loss in two spaces: an sRGB/display-referred composite (matches what the compositor views, your current term) and a scene-linear composite compressed with the same asinh. Edge pixels blended in display space and in linear space differ visibly (dark fringes arise from gamma-space blending), so supervising the linear composite directly targets the fringe/halo failure a compositor sees.
- INFERENCE: Spill penalty options (none published): (1) a chroma-excess term, penalize predicted FG's green-minus-max(red, blue) (for green screens, the channel the despill suppresses) in excess of the GT FG's value, weighted by alpha > 0 and heavier on the edge band; (2) a "plate-leak" term using the synthetic data: train with composites where the plate is the GT FG plus varying amounts of spill contribution, so GT excludes spill by construction (you already do this); (3) an FBA-style exclusion term between predicted FG and the estimated plate background gradient to stop screen texture leaking into FG edges. All hypotheses, require A/B.
- INFERENCE: Weight FG loss by alpha (soft) rather than hard alpha > 0, so near-zero-alpha pixels (where straight color is ill-defined and noisy) do not dominate; use the unpremultiplied color only where alpha is above a small threshold or weight its error by alpha^p. This is a standard concern for straight FG but is not directly sourced.

### Gaps
- No matting paper comparing transfer functions (linear vs sRGB vs mu-law vs asinh) for FG/composite losses. No published spill-residual loss. Ke et al.'s exact per-task numbers beyond "2 to 9 dB" not retrieved.

## 5. Evaluation: metrics that track human and professional judgement

### Takeaway
Pixel metrics SAD and MSE correlate poorly with perceived quality of mattes; the classic perceptual study showed rank correlations of only 0.28 to 0.51 for SAD/MSE, versus 0.75 for purpose-built gradient and connectivity errors on their matching error types. In video, temporal coherence outranks accuracy for slow motion. Professional QA asks about edge chatter/boiling, fringing/halos, despill balance, core holes, motion-blur softness, all judged in composite over several backgrounds with levels pushed. Recommendation: composite-based headline metric plus targeted artifact metrics plus a temporal metric plus human spot checks.

### Cited Findings
- Rhemann et al. 2009 (perceptually motivated benchmark): user study with ranking of compositions; example where average user ranking was exactly opposite to SAD/MSE (SAD 312 vs 83, and 1215 vs 806 in two examples); Kendall tau correlations with average user ranking: on the gradient test set Grad 0.75, Conn 0.47, MSE 0.51, SAD 0.45; on the connectivity set Conn 0.75, Grad 0.40, MSE 0.34, SAD 0.28; individual-user agreement with average user 0.87 (gradient set) and 0.90 (connectivity set); 12% of participants preferred the visually worse-by-majority composite in one pair, suggesting inherent ambiguity. Focus was compositing onto a new homogeneous background. Error classes that degrade quality most: connectivity errors (disconnected pieces such as floating hair) and gradient errors (over-smoothing or erroneous discontinuities). — [Rhemann et al. 2009](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2009/01/cvpr09-matting-Eval.pdf)
- Master's thesis (Rott, TU Wien) with 17 participants and 30 image crops: pixel-wise measures "do not correlate well" with visual quality; unconnected objects rated poorly; sharp spots rated worse than soft transitions; findings on foreground-vs-background artifact severity were not confirmed, with two groups of observers. — [Rott thesis](https://resolver.obvsg.at/urn:nbn:at:at-ubtuw:1-25887)
- Eye-tracking study by Rhemann: considerable correlation between photography expertise and ability to detect matting errors; scan paths differ by experience. This supports calibrating human review to expert compositors. — [Rhemann et al. 2009 SPIE](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.805379)
- Deep Image Matting noted that standard error metrics may not reflect real-image preference (a method top on alphamatting.com was preferred on few real images). — [Deep Image Matting](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Xu_Deep_Image_Matting_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)
- Video: Erofeev et al. as in Section 2. The benchmark composes extracted objects over challenging backgrounds, using green-screen capture plus a reflection-aware stop-motion technique for GT. — [Erofeev et al. abstract](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2015/papers/paper099/abstract099.pdf)
- RVM-style evaluation: MAD, MSE, Grad, Conn for alpha, dtSSD for temporal, FG MSE only where GT alpha > 0; Conn dropped at high resolution because it is too expensive. — [RVM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.11515)
- MatAnyone 2's MQE-derived ERR/MER/BER for no-GT footage (Section 3).
- VFX QA practice: key tech-check items include matte holes, "wobbling or inconsistent edges", rogue matte shapes, dark or light edges, retained detail in hair/semi-transparent/defocused/motion-blurred areas, despill balance; "edges 1:1 with the plate: softness, despill, no boil/halos, motion blur matching shutter"; expose up and down to catch floating greys and verify levels; view the alpha directly to find holes in the foreground and noisy solid areas; preview at full resolution in full motion against a bright primary color; a hard core matte plus an overlaid edge matte as the standard two-pass strategy (the user's "holes" and "leak" metrics map to this). — [Keheka tech check](https://www.keheka.com/tech-check-checklist/), [CompStart tech check](https://compstart.pro/tech-check), [Keying chapter excerpt](https://flylib.com/books/en/2.104.1/blue_screen_and_green_screen_keying.html), [Keying and matte extraction summary](https://learnlyai.co.uk/en/course-hub/design/3d-design-animation/visual-effects-vfx/keying-and-matte-extraction-techniques) (last item is a low-authority course page; quality checklist lines: no edge chatter, motion blur coverage, no sudden shape change)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Keep composite MAE over multiple new backgrounds as the headline, but (a) compute it in scene-linear after an asinh/tone-mapped transform and in display space, (b) include HDR highlight and dark backgrounds, (c) report per-band (core, edge band, background) so a low overall number cannot hide edge errors, since edge pixels are a tiny fraction of the frame.
- INFERENCE: Add the Rhemann-type metrics that track humans: Grad (already) and a connectivity metric or a cheaper surrogate (connected-component of alpha > threshold areas outside the main subject counted as floating fragments; the "leak" metric partly covers this). Conn is expensive at 4K, so compute on edge-band crops.
- INFERENCE: Add flow-aware temporal metrics beyond dtSSD: MESSDdt with GT flow from synthetic clean plates, stratified by motion speed, plus an "edge chatter" metric = high-frequency temporal energy of alpha in the static-subject edge band (power in frame-to-frame differences where GT difference is zero). dtSSD alone penalizes both real motion and errors; the Erofeev conclusion says humans punish incoherence most for slow motion.
- INFERENCE: Add a spill/fringe metric: chroma excess (screen-channel minus max of other channels) of predicted FG versus GT FG in the edge band, and "composite halo" measured as signed luminance error of the composite over a dark and over a bright background (separate signs flag dark-fringe vs light-fringe, which the checklist calls out).
- INFERENCE: Report holes as the count/area of GT-opaque pixels with predicted alpha < 1 - epsilon at several epsilons (0.01, 0.05, 0.2), to separate semantic holes from precision loss (Section 6).
- INFERENCE: Human protocol: blind A/B on short composite clips (looped, full-res crops, over graded backgrounds with pushed levels), with 3+ compositors, report win rate with confidence intervals and use as ground truth to calibrate the learned critic (Spearman/Kendall between critic score and win rate). Rhemann's 87% to 90% individual-to-mean agreement is the reasonable ceiling to expect from per-pair agreement.

### Gaps
- No post-2015 study found that correlates modern matting metrics (including composite MAE, Conn, Grad, dtSSD) with human judgement for deep-learning video mattes; I could not verify claims in the 2020s literature beyond MatAnyone 2's qualitative statement that MQE metric trends align with reference metrics. No quantitative VFX-industry matte-quality standard (thresholds) found; the industry sources are checklists. Erofeev's per-metric correlation values not retrieved.

## 6. Precision: bf16/fp16 and loss on near-1 alpha (holes)

### Takeaway
No matting-specific precision study was found. From first principles, bf16 cannot represent alpha values close to 1 finely (see inference), so alpha must be produced and compared in fp32 or fp16 at the output stage; PyTorch's AMP guidance supports keeping reductions and loss in float32. A practitioner report (low authority) shows bf16 silently producing blocky high-resolution masks when spatial coordinates were stored in bf16.

### Cited Findings
- PyTorch AMP: autocast should wrap forward and loss computation; "ops like reductions often require the dynamic range of float32"; fp16 needs GradScaler to avoid gradient underflow; most bf16-pretrained models cannot run in fp16's range (max 65504) and overflow. — [torch.amp docs](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/amp.md)
- A practitioner report (LinkedIn post, Sept 2026, unverified, single anecdote) describes high-res segmentation masks becoming "terribly blocky" because image-space coordinates (0 to 1) were stored in bfloat16, which has float32's range but very low mantissa precision. Fix was keeping coordinates in fp32. — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/corey-senger_i-ran-into-a-problem-this-week-where-bfloat16-activity-7501742077790265345-KBBJ)
- FBA found clamping/clipping of alpha mattered: adding clip_alpha lowered SAD 32.8 to 31.2 and MSE 7.2 to 6.9 on Composition-1k (see Section 1). Also found batch size 1 with GroupNorm was better for alpha precision. — [FBA Matting](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.07711v1)
- MatAnyone 2 notes MQE trained with focal loss to counter class imbalance where reliable pixels dominate; same imbalance logic applies to alpha holes (few error pixels among many opaque pixels). — [Supplementary](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Yang_MatAnyone_2_Scaling_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE (arithmetic from the bf16 format, not sourced): bf16 has 8 significant bits (7 stored), so representable values in [0.5, 1) are spaced 2^-8 = 0.0039 apart; the largest value below 1 is 0.996. Any alpha stored in bf16 therefore quantizes to steps of about 1/256 near opaque, which is invisible in 8-bit display but visible as banding/holes at 1% to 2% levels in a pushed-levels QA view and in 16-bit EXR deliveries. fp16 has 11 significant bits (spacing 2^-11 = 0.00049 in [0.5, 1)). Recommendation: final alpha head and all loss computation in fp32 (or at least fp16); bf16 elsewhere is fine. Gradients near 1 are also coarse in bf16, hurting the last fraction of a percent of convergence.
- INFERENCE: For holes (alpha slightly below 1 in solids) add a dedicated term: L1 or log-style loss on (1 - alpha) in GT-opaque regions, e.g. -log(alpha_clamped) or a hinge max(0, 1 - alpha - eps) with weight about as large as the main L1 inside the core, because L1 error of 0.01 over a large area contributes little to total L1 yet is visible in pushed levels. Optionally predict alpha through a logit head with BCE on the near-binary core, which has better gradients near 0 and 1 than L1 on a sigmoid output (INFERENCE; BiRefNet uses BCE for segmentation).
- INFERENCE: Evaluate hole metrics at multiple epsilons and on a log scale so precision-related regressions (bf16 vs fp32) are detectable; do one A/B of fp32 vs bf16 output head and keep whichever the hole metric prefers.

### Gaps
- No matting paper quantifying fp16/bf16 vs fp32 effects found. The LinkedIn report is anecdotal and not peer reviewed. No evidence found for specific near-1 alpha loss functions in matting literature (my suggestions are untested).

## 7. Synthesis: recommended training loss set, critic, and evaluation protocol

### Takeaway
Keep the proven backbone (L1 + 5-level Laplacian + composition + alpha-weighted FG L1 + temporal difference L1), drop any separate gradient loss, add a hole-focused near-opaque term, a linear-space composite term, and a spill penalty, and use a CorridorKey-specific learned critic mainly for filtering/fusing pseudo-labels on real footage. Evaluate with composite-based, band-stratified, flow-aware metrics plus periodic blinded compositor review.

### Cited Findings
- See Sections 1 to 6; every item below rests on them. In particular: Laplacian and composition helped and gradient hurt (FBA); dtSSD-style loss helped while flow-warped loss hurt (Zhang et al.); MQE-masked losses plus 0.1 critic loss gave MAD 1.99 to 1.90, MSE 0.71 to 0.62 (MatAnyone 2); display-encoded training beat linear by 2 to 9 dB (Ke et al.); SAD/MSE correlate at 0.28 to 0.51 vs 0.75 for targeted metrics (Rhemann).

### Inferences
Proposed concrete loss set (INFERENCE, starting values to be tuned; all weights relative to alpha L1 = 1):
- Alpha: L1 (1.0), 5-level Laplacian with level weights 2^(s-1)/5 (1.0), optional edge-weighted L1 with 1 + 5 x edge-indicator (try as A/B), no separate gradient loss.
- Hole/core term: L1 on (1 - alpha) in GT-alpha >= 0.99 pixels (0.5 to 1.0), plus a mirrored term on the leak side (alpha in GT-alpha <= 0.01) so leak is not under-weighted.
- Composition: sRGB composite L1 over random backgrounds (1.0, existing) plus an asinh-compressed scene-linear composite L1 (0.5), backgrounds sampled including saturated and dark plates; also include random plate-spill augmentation so the GT excludes spill.
- Foreground: alpha-weighted L1 in asinh space (1.0 to 2.0 on edges), plus spill-excess penalty on the screen channel in edge band (0.1 to 0.5, tune), optional FBA exclusion term at low weight (0.25 of the FG block).
- Temporal: L1 on alpha frame-difference with weight 1 to 5, masked to pixels reliable in both frames; same for FG difference in asinh space; no flow-warped training loss at edges; optionally a flow-warped consistency on opaque core for real clips.
- Auxiliary heads: keep base head and /2 head with down-weighting (0.25 to 0.5 each), consistent with MODNet's multi-branch ablation and FBA's 0.25 weighting of secondary heads.
- Schedule (adapting MatAnyone 1 and RVM): stage 1 low-res/short clips with full loss at higher LR; stage 2 longer sequences, lower LR (about 10x lower), turning on the core-focused and critic-masked losses; stage 3 high-res crops (large patches help, per AEMatter 1024 patches) with lowest LR (about 100x lower than stage 1); ramp the temporal and hole terms up over the first part of each stage to avoid early over-smoothing. Final alpha head and losses in fp32.
- Real unlabeled clips: teacher/ensemble pseudo-alpha and FG, fused by the CorridorKey critic as in MatAnyone 2 (pixel-wise union of reliable regions), losses masked by reliability, critic penalty weight 0.1. Add a self-consistency term (MODNet SOC style) on real clips: composite over a new background and require output invariant under brightness and background changes of the plate.
- Evaluation protocol (headline plus diagnostics): composite MAE over at least 4 held-out backgrounds (headline, in both display and tone-mapped linear), band-stratified (core/edge/background), plus SAD, MSE, Grad, Conn on edge-band crops, holes at three epsilons, leak, FG MAE and chroma-excess in edge band, dtSSD, MESSDdt with GT flow stratified by motion speed, edge-chatter energy, halo sign metric, and for real footage the critic ERR/MER/BER plus periodic blind A/B by compositors for the final checkpoint selection and for calibrating the critic.

### Gaps
- All specific numeric weights above are my starting guesses, not published values; the published numbers I could confirm are the Laplacian weights, RVM's x5 temporal weight, MatAnyone's 0.1 critic weight, 1.5x boundary weight, FBA's 0.25 F/B block weight, and BiRefNet's 30/0.5/10/5. Whether the critic-masked loss still helps at HDR/hair-scale detail, and whether it can be gamed, is untested.
