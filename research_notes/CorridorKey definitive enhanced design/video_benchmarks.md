# Video matting benchmarks and competing models for ranking an enhanced green-screen keyer (as of 2026-10-21)

Research budget: about 25 tool calls (fetch/search). Several items could not be verified; see Gaps. No em dashes used by design.

## 1. Public video matting benchmarks: access, protocol, metrics, current numbers

### Takeaway
The de facto public protocol is the MatAnyone/MatAnyone 2 table: VideoMatte240K-test and YouTubeMatte at 512x288 and 1920x1080 (MAD, MSE, Grad, dtSSD, Conn), plus the 19-video CRGNN real set. MatAnyone 2 (CVPR 2026) is the best published number on all of them among the compared methods. The VideoMatte/YouTubeMatte sets use first-frame masks for mask-guided methods, so a keyer can only join the comparison by running the same composites. CorridorKey's own "QualityBenchmark" on Hugging Face is NOT a metric benchmark (just inputs/outputs zips).

### Cited Findings
- VideoMatte240K-test (RVM protocol) composites are downloadable as `videomatte_512x288.tar` and `videomatte_1920x1080.tar`: https://robustvideomatting.blob.core.windows.net/eval/videomatte_512x288.tar and https://robustvideomatting.blob.core.windows.net/eval/videomatte_1920x1080.tar. The RVM page labels the first archive "512x512" in text but the URL says 512x288 (verify after download). Backgrounds are DVM background clips (first 100 frames, human-free); image backgrounds are the BGMv2 validation set (~200). Only the VideoMatte240K composite set is shipped; D646 and AIM must be obtained from authors and composited with the scripts in `/evaluation`. The page names no metrics and no numbers. — [RVM training.md](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md)
- VideoMatte240K: 484 high-resolution green-screen videos, 240,709 unique frames of alpha and foreground, made in After Effects (so GT is itself keyer-derived, an approximation). — [Papers with Code summary (via search)](https://astro.paperswithcode.com/dataset/videomatte240k); [BGMv2 repo](https://github.com/PeterL1n/BackgroundMattingV2) says VideoMatte240K was published April 21, 2021 and is downloadable from https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/background-matting-v2/#/datasets
- YouTubeMatte (MatAnyone): 32 foregrounds from YouTube videos, harmonized with Harmonizer before compositing; released as a download with 512x288 and 1920x1080 versions, first-frame masks, and MatAnyone inference results. VideoMatte240K-Test in their protocol: 5 foregrounds from purchased footage, not harmonized; first-frame masks provided, dataset itself obtained via RVM docs. — [MatAnyone repo](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone)
- MatAnyone 2 (arXiv 2512.11782, CVPR 2026 Highlight) tables (lower is better; MAD/MSE x1e3 scale as printed): — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
  - VideoMatte 512x288, MAD: RVM-Large 5.32, GVM 6.39, FTP-VM 6.13, MaGGIe 5.49, MatAnyone 5.15, MatAnyone 2 4.73 (MSE 0.55, Grad 0.51, dtSSD 1.12, Conn 0.20).
  - VideoMatte 1920x1080, MAD: RVM-Large 5.81, GVM 6.33, AdaM 4.42, FTP-VM 8.00, MaGGIe 4.42, MatAnyone 4.24, MatAnyone 2 4.10 (MSE 0.28, Grad 3.45, dtSSD 1.15).
  - YouTubeMatte 512x288, MAD: RVM-Large 3.36, GVM 3.30, FTP-VM 3.08, MaGGIe 3.54, MatAnyone 2.72, MatAnyone 2 2.30 (MSE 0.78, Grad 0.78, dtSSD 1.45, Conn 0.32).
  - YouTubeMatte 1920x1080, MAD: RVM-Large 3.58, GVM 2.68, MaGGIe 2.37, MatAnyone 1.99, MatAnyone 2 1.61 (MSE 0.50, Grad 7.14, dtSSD 1.53). RVM-Large 1080p: MSE 1.23, Grad 12.97, dtSSD 2.04.
  - CRGNN real-world set (19 videos, GT every 10th frame), MAD/MSE/Grad/dtSSD: RVM-Large 5.75/2.47/13.26/5.17; GVM 5.03/2.15/14.28/4.86; MaGGIe 9.50/6.11/16.51/6.02; MatAnyone 5.76/3.04/15.55/5.44; MatAnyone 2 4.24/2.00/11.74/4.54.
  - A GT-free real-world table (MAD/MSE against a reference, dtSSD, ERR/MER/BER) is also reported; MatAnyone 2 dtSSD 0.99, ERR 0.46, MER 1.13, BER 15.19. Note GVM scores poorly there (MAD 10.65), flagging that GT-free metrics are method-sensitive.
- MatAnyone 2 compares mask-guided methods under a first-frame mask; MaGGIe needs per-frame masks. Conn is only reported at 512x288. — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- VMReal (28K clips, 2.4M frames, real footage) is a TRAINING set (Table 4 compares dataset sizes: VM108 108 clips/92K frames, VideoMatte240K 484/240K, VM800 826/320K); the dataset is not released (unchecked TODO in the repo). It is not a test benchmark. — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2); [MatAnyone2 repo](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone2)
- VideoMaMa (arXiv 2601.14255, CVPR 2026) first-frame protocol on V-HIM60 (Easy/Medium/Hard) and YouTubeMatte 1080p, MAD/MAD-T/MSE/Grad: YouTubeMatte: SAM2 3.853/103.2/2.613/37.41; MatAnyone 1.991/51.09/0.709/8.90; SAM2+VideoMaMa 1.356/35.51/0.294/3.57; SAM2-Matte 1.270/33.53/0.283/3.46. V-HIM60 Hard: MatAnyone 5.72 MAD, SAM2+VideoMaMa 2.78, SAM2-Matte 2.61. The MatAnyone baseline row (1.99/0.71/8.90) is identical to the MatAnyone 2 paper's, so scales match and VideoMaMa-based pipelines (MAD 1.27 to 1.36) beat MatAnyone 2 (1.61) on YouTubeMatte 1080p, though with a mask-guided SAM2 pipeline and not head-to-head in one paper. MA-V (50K real videos) not yet released. — [VideoMaMa paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14255v1); [VideoMaMa repo](https://github.com/cvlab-kaist/VideoMaMa)
- V-HIM2K5 (train) and V-HIM60 (val/test) from MaGGIe: hosted at https://huggingface.co/datasets/chuonghm/MaGGIe-HIM (V-HIM2K5 in 7 zip parts, V-HIM60 single zip), composed from VideoMatte240K, VM108 and CRGNN with `tools/synthesize_video_him.py`; masks from GenVIS + XMem. Intended for multi-person instance matting, so only partly relevant to green screen. — [MaGGIe DATASET.md](https://github.com/hmchuong/MaGGIe/blob/main/docs/DATASET.md)
- VideoMatting108 (VM108) was built from green-screen footage with GT extracted via After Effects and Primatte Studio; Video Matting Benchmark (Erofeev et al. 2015, videomatting.com) combines chroma-keyed and stop-motion sequences and accepts submissions (alpha + FG sequences uploaded by form); its leaderboard is old (FBA Matting 1.3, Background Matting 2.5) and tests trimap-based/clean-plate methods; it has accuracy and temporal-coherency metrics using GT optical flow. — [search result: PwC/arXiv summaries](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11208); [videomatting.com](https://videomatting.com/)
- Magenta Green Screen (2023) argues deep-learning-era GT derives from chroma-key approximations or rotoscoping and records more accurate GT. — [arXiv 2306.13702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13702)
- CorridorKey QualityBenchmark: only two zips (~5.28 GB) of inputs and outputs from CorridorKey 1.0 to "test results against the original source quality"; no metrics, protocol, or leaderboard; license line apache-2.0 but metadata empty (unconfirmed). — [HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nikopueringer/CorridorKey1.0_QualityBenchmark)
- Metric definitions used by the community (MAD, MSE, Grad, Conn, dtSSD) are as in the tables above; MESSDdt was not found in any fetched source.

### Inferences
- Our own set remains the only benchmark with exact GT under realistic green-screen degradations (spill, codecs, HDR); all public sets have GT derived from keyer or roto, which biases toward keyer-like outputs and caps achievable error. Reporting public numbers is for credibility and comparability only.
- To appear on the RVM/MatAnyone tables, run on the same 512x288 and 1080p composites (those are green-screen-free composites, so a keyer must be fed the foreground-over-background composite, which is out of distribution for it; consider also feeding it a synthesized-green version of the same FG, clearly labeled).
- GT-free real-world metrics (ERR/MER/BER) are unstable across methods; prefer CRGNN (GT every 10 frames) for real footage.

## 2. Competitor models runnable locally (Linux, RTX A6000 48 GB)

### Takeaway
Fairest field: CorridorKey 1.0 (green-screen-native, alpha hint), RVM (no input), BGMv2 (clean plate, fits green screen), MatAnyone and MatAnyone 2 (first-frame mask), GVM (no mask), VideoMaMa/SAM2-Matte (mask-guided, diffusion, heavy), MaGGIe (per-frame masks), FTP-VM (trimap), BiRefNet-matting (per frame, no temporal), plus classical keyers (ffmpeg chromakey/colorkey, pymatting with a trimap). "CorridorKey 2.0" could not be verified to exist.

### Cited Findings
- CorridorKey 1.0: repo https://github.com/nikopueringer/CorridorKey ; no releases listed and no 2.0 mention; weights `CorridorKey_v1.0.safetensors` (auto-download from HF) and `CorridorKeyBlue_1.0.safetensors`; input = RGB green/blue frame (sRGB or linear) + coarse alpha hint (from rough chroma key, GVM, VideoMaMa, BiRefNet); trained on coarse/eroded hints, handles over-expanded hints poorly; native 2048x2048 backbone, scales to 4K; about 10 GB VRAM at 2048 on NVIDIA; EXR 16/32 float supported; license = variation of CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (commercial use of output allowed; repackaging/paid APIs need agreement; derivatives must keep the Corridor Key name); no speed numbers published. — [GitHub](https://github.com/nikopueringer/CorridorKey)
- No "CorridorKey 2.0" release found; web search returned only 1.0 (March 2026), Beeble cloud integration (April 2026), and forks (CorridorKey-Engine async multi-GPU, EZ-CorridorKey, CorridorKey-Runtime, FCP/MLX plugin). — [search](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/04/22/beeble-adds-corridorkey-green-screen-model/); [CorridorKey-Engine](https://gittrend.io/repo/99oblivius/CorridorKey-Engine)
- RVM: GPL-3.0; MobileNetV3 and ResNet50 weights (v1.0.0 release; TorchScript/ONNX/CoreML too); input = video or frames, no mask/trimap, recurrent; `downsample_ratio` must be tuned (0.25 for HD, 0.125 for 4K in the speed test); tensor throughput 172 FPS HD / 154 FPS 4K on RTX 3090 FP16 (end-to-end script much slower). — [RVM repo](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting)
- BGMv2: MIT; needs a captured clean background plate (exactly the green-screen-compatible use); 4K 30 fps and HD 60 fps on RTX 2080 Ti; weights on release v1.0.0; not temporal (per-frame). — [BGMv2 repo](https://github.com/PeterL1n/BackgroundMattingV2)
- MatAnyone: NTU S-Lab License 1.0 (non-commercial, read LICENSE); input = video + first-frame PNG mask (SAM2 demo or Gradio clicks); `--max_size` optional downsample, no default limit; weights `matanyone.pth` (HF PeiqingYang/MatAnyone). — [MatAnyone repo](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone)
- MatAnyone 2: same license (NTU S-Lab 1.0), same inputs, weights `matanyone2.pth` (HF PeiqingYang/MatAnyone2), VRAM not stated, last push around 2026-03-16. — [MatAnyone2 repo](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone2); [gittrend](https://gittrend.io/repo/pq-yang/MatAnyone2)
- GVM (Generative Video Matting, SIGGRAPH 2025): BSD-2-Clause academic (commercial: contact Chunhua Shen); HF weights `geyongtao/gvm`; demo takes a video, no mask (`--mode matte`); default `--max_resolution 960`, 8 frames/batch, 1 denoise step; CorridorKey README says GVM needs about 80 GB (unmodified). — [GVM repo](https://github.com/aim-uofa/GVM); [CorridorKey README](https://github.com/nikopueringer/CorridorKey)
- VideoMaMa: code CC BY-NC 4.0, checkpoint under Stability AI Community License; weights HF `SammyLim/VideoMaMa` plus `stable-video-diffusion-img2vid-xt` base; inputs = frame folder + mask folder (per-frame masks; the paper also does first-frame via SAM2); CorridorKey README says it natively needs 80 GB+ VRAM (community sub-24 GB optimizations exist elsewhere); SAM2-Matte is the SAM2 fine-tune on MA-V (release status of weights unverified). — [VideoMaMa repo](https://github.com/cvlab-kaist/VideoMaMa)
- MaGGIe: CC BY-NC 4.0; per-frame instance binary masks as guidance; model zoo on HF; no speed/VRAM given (video eval runs on a single GPU). — [MaGGIe repo](https://github.com/hmchuong/MaGGIe)
- FTP-VM: GPL-3.0 (weights may be limited by training data); requires a thumbnail + trimap memory pair (not just mask); 1024x576 at 40 FPS on RTX 2080 Ti; weight download location not stated. — [FTP-VM repo](https://github.com/csvt32745/ftp-vm)
- VMFormer (WACV 2024): paper only found; code/weights/license unconfirmed. — [arXiv 2208.12801](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12801)
- BiRefNet: MIT; BiRefNet-matting (trimap-free) and BiRefNet_HR-matting (2048x2048) exist, per-frame, no temporal modeling; about 3.5 to 5.5 GB at 1024 (FP16 to FP32); 17 FPS at 1024 on RTX 4090 FP16; video notebook exists. — [BiRefNet repo](https://github.com/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet)
- SAM 2 / 2.1: Apache 2.0; four checkpoints; 39.5 to 91.2 FPS on A100; binary masks only (use as the mask provider for MatAnyone-class models); SAM 3 not confirmed from the page. — [SAM2 repo](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2)
- pymatting: MIT; closed-form and KNN alpha matting + closed-form/multilevel foreground estimation; needs a single-channel float64 trimap (0 bg, 1 fg, else unknown); per-frame; slow first import (Numba compile). — [pymatting](https://github.com/pymatting/pymatting)
- Omnimatte / Generative Omnimatte: layer decomposition (objects plus shadows/reflections), per-video optimization (Omnimatte) or diffusion (Generative Omnimatte CVPR 2025); license reported Apache 2.0 for Omnimatte by a secondary source only; not a keyer. — [Omnimatte paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06993); [Generative Omnimatte](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16683v2)
- DaVinci Resolve: Python scripting API works with `-nogui` headless launch; no documentation found that Magic Mask can be driven through the API (unverified; likely GUI only). — [Resolve API readme](https://resolvedevdoc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/readme_resolveapi.html); [headless note via search](https://wiki.dvresolve.com/developer-docs/scripting-api)
- Beeble Background Remover offers CorridorKey and MatAnyone, cloud only. — [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/04/22/beeble-adds-corridorkey-green-screen-model/)

### Inferences
- Fair input policy per method (what to feed each competitor): RVM: nothing. BGMv2: the exact clean plate from our simulated stage. MatAnyone/MatAnyone 2/SAM2-Matte/VideoMaMa: first-frame mask thresholded from GT alpha (state it; also run a degraded-mask variant, since a GT mask is generous). GVM: nothing. MaGGIe: GT-derived per-frame masks (upper bound, label as such). FTP-VM: trimap from GT by dilation/erosion band. CorridorKey: alpha hint from a rough chroma key AND, as an oracle row, from eroded GT. BiRefNet: raw frames.
- Two licenses (CorridorKey CC BY-NC-SA variant, MatAnyone S-Lab, VideoMaMa/MaGGIe CC BY-NC) are non-commercial, fine for research ranking, but redistribution of side-by-sides containing their output should be checked.
- A6000 48 GB can run everything except unmodified GVM/VideoMaMa at full settings (80 GB claims); use reduced resolution (GVM `--max_resolution 960` default) and note the downscale in the table.
- Classical baselines (not sourced here, from general knowledge, mark as such): ffmpeg `chromakey`/`colorkey`/`despill` filters, Natron's Keyer/IBK/Primatte-style nodes, OpenCV HSV/YCbCr threshold with spill suppression. Include one tuned-per-clip and one fixed-parameter baseline.

## 3. Recommended benchmark suite, protocol, and fair presentation

### Takeaway
Report three tiers: (A) our 15-shot exact-GT green-screen set as the headline, (B) the public MatAnyone-protocol sets (VideoMatte240K-test and YouTubeMatte at 512x288 and 1080p, plus CRGNN) for external comparability, (C) qualitative real green-screen clips including the CorridorKey QualityBenchmark zips. Use MAD, MSE, Grad, dtSSD (and Conn at low res) on public sets; our extra metrics on ours; present with synchronized side-by-sides, zoomed crops, difference videos, and xt-slices.

### Cited Findings
- Public-protocol metrics and resolutions to match: MAD, MSE, Grad, dtSSD (all resolutions), Conn (512x288 only) — [MatAnyone 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v2)
- Mask-guided fair-comparison convention in recent papers: first-frame mask for MatAnyone/VideoMaMa-style, all-frame masks for MaGGIe, with synthetic degradations (8x/32x downsample, polygon easy/hard) to simulate imperfect user masks — [VideoMaMa](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14255v1)
- videomatting.com measures temporal coherency using optical flow computed on GT sequences so metrics don't favor methods sharing a motion estimator — [videomatting.com](https://videomatting.com/)
- Chroma-key-derived GT is an approximation and biases evaluations — [Magenta Green Screen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13702); [VideoMatting benchmark summary](https://videomatting.com/)

### Inferences (recommendations, not sourced facts)
- Protocol: same frames, same resolution, same composite backgrounds for all; fix random seeds for backgrounds; report per-shot and macro-average; bootstrap 95% CIs over shots; give the exact input each method received (table in section 2 inferences); report both "GT-mask oracle" and "practical mask (SAM2/rough key)" rows for mask-guided methods; separate rows for green-screen-native vs generic methods.
- Our metrics: composite MAE over new backgrounds is the primary (it is what compositors see); keep edge-band MAE, holes, leak, FG error; add dtSSD with GT-flow-warped temporal error (warp error E_warp using GT flow from the foreground motion), and a flicker metric as temporal std of alpha in static GT regions.
- Public-set caveat: GT comes from roto/keyer, so keyer-like models can look better than reality; also composites have no green spill, so a keyer needs the synthetic green version for a fair test.
- Presentation: (1) synchronized side-by-side grid, same crop and playback sync, labels hidden or randomized for a user preference study; (2) fixed zoomed crops on hair, motion blur, semitransparent cloth, with nearest-neighbor 4x upscale; (3) per-method |alpha - GT| heatmap video with a shared color scale; (4) flicker visualization: per-pixel temporal variance map or frame difference of alpha, same scale; (5) xt-slices: stack one row (or a column) through a hair edge across all 40 frames to show temporal jitter, one panel per method plus GT; (6) composite over a contrasting new background and over a checkerboard; (7) lossless or high-bitrate (CRF 12) encodes, same codec for all, and also show the failure shots, not only wins.
- Run the unsourced classical baselines (ffmpeg chromakey + despill, tuned and untuned) so claims against "a compositor's keyer" are defensible.

### Gaps
- No published standard for green-screen video matting visualization or for a temporal flicker metric specific to matting beyond dtSSD and (older) GT-flow coherency; recommendations above are our design.
